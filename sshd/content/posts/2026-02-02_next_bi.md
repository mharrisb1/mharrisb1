# What comes next in BI?

Given that I work on a vertically-integrated data stack, I always have my ear to the ground on the data industry at large. A big piece of that industry is traditional business intelligence (BI).

BI has been around for decades and the most popular tools and solutions such as Tableau, PowerBI, etc. are really showing their age. There was a renaissance with the "modern data stack" in the late 2010s early early 2020s but even with all of the new offerings, I see traditional "create a dashboard for execs/leaders that they review periodically" as antiquated. Even without LLMs, this was tired.

My not so shocking prediction for the future is **"business intelligence" gets swallowed up by "operational automation"**.

## Shifting Left on Data

The key enabler of this shift is moving data ownership out of centralized teams and into the hands of the domain experts who actually understand it. Just like DevOps moved operational responsibility to the teams that write the code, data needs to follow the same pattern. When a centralized data team is an afterthought translating requirements from the rest of the business, you get stale dashboards that nobody trusts.

When domain teams own their data, a few things happen naturally:

- **Decisions move closer to real-time.** The people with the most context are working directly with live data instead of waiting on a quarterly report.
- **Tooling fits the problem.** Teams that own their data tend to build custom operational UIs and automation instead of forcing everything into a general-purpose BI tool. The tool shapes the organization — if your BI tool is limited and clunky, you will be too.
- **Data becomes operational, not just analytical.** Instead of looking backwards at what happened, teams build automated triggers, alerts, and workflows that act on data as it arrives.

## Where Do LLMs Fit In?

LLMs are an accelerant here, not the driver. The shift from BI to operational automation was already underway before LLMs entered the picture. But they do unlock new capabilities — natural language self-serve querying, summarization, anomaly explanation, and early ideas around "context graphs" that are still being fleshed out but directionally make sense.
